About me

Hi, I’m
Ayushi.I’m rarely still.

I’m based in Kolkata, India, and currently studying Information Technology at the University of Calcutta.

But a degree does not really explain what I do. There is almost always something I’m learning, something I’m organising, something I’m designing, something I’m researching, something I’m creating, or some completely new thing I’ve decided I want to understand.

I’ve never really wanted to be good at just one thing. Not because I think I can master everything, but because I genuinely enjoy becoming capable at something I could not do before.

That is probably the simplest way to explain me. ↘

Before university

Where the
curiosity started.

My interest in computers started in school, where Computer Science was one of my major academic subjects. By Class 12, a computer-related field had become one of my priorities for university.

But my university journey did not begin exactly where I wanted it to. I initially joined the Department of Optics and Optoelectronics at the University of Calcutta. Information Technology, however, had always been the direction I wanted to move towards.

So during my first year, I worked towards making that change. Eventually, I upgraded to the Information Technology department.

For me, that was more than simply changing departments. It was the first time university really taught me that if there is somewhere you want to be, you can work towards getting there instead of simply accepting where you started.

Committees, hall activities and prize-related events taught me to work through ambiguity, not around it. They also kept bringing me back to design—the visual language that helps an event feel coherent before it even begins.

Design

I do not just design
sometimes. I design
almost everything.

Brand identities. Social creatives. Event graphics. Posters. Promotional material. Presentations. Visual systems. The details that make something feel like it belongs together.

I consider myself a brand designer, and whenever I get the opportunity to design something, I usually take it. I’ve designed for university events, fests, committees, organisations, and different things I’ve been involved with over time.

Sometimes I’m designing because someone specifically asked me to. Sometimes I’m designing because I looked at something and thought, “This could look much better.”

I enjoy the entire process: understanding the identity of something, figuring out how it should communicate, choosing how it should look, building the visual language, and then seeing someone else use something I created. For me, design is not simply decoration. It is communication.

Building & creating

I like being involved
in the whole process.

I participate in hackathons and build things because I like taking an idea from nothing to something tangible.

But I do not enjoy being restricted to just the technical side. I like thinking about the problem. I like designing the solution. I like figuring out how it should work. I like building it.

I like presenting it. I like explaining it. And I like hearing someone challenge the idea and having to rethink it.

That is also why pitching has become something I enjoy. There is something fascinating about taking an idea that makes perfect sense inside your own head and trying to make it make sense inside somebody else’s.

Research & academia

What?Why?Another way?

One of my biggest goals now is higher education. I’ve realised that I genuinely love academia. I like studying. I like reading. I like asking questions. And I have a particular problem with stopping at the first answer.

If I understand what something does, I immediately want to know why. If I understand why, I want to know whether there is another way. And if there is another way, I want to know which one is better and why.

That curiosity is what draws me towards research. I’ve started writing academic papers and exploring research alongside everything else I’m doing. I do not know exactly where that path will take me yet, but I know I want to pursue higher studies and spend more time in an environment where asking difficult questions is encouraged rather than inconvenient.

Haemologix.

Among everything I’ve explored, Haemologix is particularly special to me. I co-founded it with Aftab, and unlike many things that begin as an interesting idea, this one came from a personal experience.

It became an opportunity to do much more than build something. It meant researching a problem, thinking about people, developing an idea, designing it, communicating it, pitching it, and figuring out how something that starts as a thought can become something tangible.

It also taught me that I do not want to choose between being a builder, designer, researcher, or entrepreneur. I want to explore all of them.

Outside technology

The other things
that make me, me.

There is a lot of my life that has nothing to do with computers. And I think that is important.

These are not separate portfolios competing for attention. They are simply other ways I have learned to observe, practise, express, make and become more capable.

Art.

I draw and sketch. Art gives me a different way of observing the world—proportions, expressions, shapes, shadows and details I might otherwise overlook. The work, the making and the recognition all belong to the same scrapbook.

Dance.

Classical dance has been one of my longer-running creative pursuits. There is no shortcut around practice. You repeat something until your body understands what your mind already knows. It has taught me discipline, patience, expression and attention to tiny details.

Fashion.

I enjoy putting together looks and experimenting with how clothes, styling and visual identity can become another form of expression. One Regency-inspired look, built from thrifted pieces and handmade details, was later featured by The Telegraph and My Kolkata.

The common thread

I do not want
to be just
one thing.

StudentDesignerBuilderResearcherFounderOrganiserDancerArtist

If you looked at different parts of my life separately, they might seem unrelated. Technology. Design. Research. Entrepreneurship. Dance. Art. Fashion. Event management. Academics.

But I do not really think of them as separate identities. They all come from the same place. I like learning. I like creating. I like figuring things out. I like being useful. I like making things better. And I like becoming capable of doing things I could not do before.

I think the most accurate description of me might be this: I’m never really still. When I’m not studying, I’m probably designing. When I’m not designing, I’m probably organising something. When I’m not organising something, I’m probably working on an idea. And when I’m finally doing none of those things, there is a good chance I’ve found something completely new that I now want to learn.

I do not think that restlessness is something I want to get rid of. It is what keeps me moving.

I do not have a perfectly linear five-year plan.
I have directions.

I want to pursue higher studies. I want to explore research more seriously. I want to keep building. I want to keep designing. I want to keep creating.

I want to keep participating, organising, pitching, experimenting, and occasionally saying yes to things I have absolutely no idea how to do yet. Because I think that is how I have grown so far—not by choosing one identity and staying inside it, but by constantly adding another layer.

I am still figuring out whatever comes next. And that is probably my favourite part.
